FROM THE DEVELOPER

Developer's Talk

The thinking behind K2 AeroSim, what's coming next, and how to report what's broken.

Thoughts

Hi! I'm Kashyap, a final-year undergraduate engineering student and, more importantly, an aerospace enthusiast.

K2 AeroSim started from a simple idea: to build a unified rocket simulation platform where multiple engineering disciplines work together through a connected multiphysics pipeline. Instead of isolated modules, K2 aims to provide an integrated environment where propulsion, aerodynamics, CFD, structures, optimization, and other analyses seamlessly exchange data, making the overall design and analysis process more streamlined and efficient.

Several modules within K2 are built upon well-established methodologies and open-source tools such as OpenRocket, SU2, and CalculiX. However, integrating these different systems into a unified workflow has been far from straightforward. At times, completely unexpected issues appeared during integration, and on more than one occasion the software managed to "break physics" in ways I didn't think were possible.

Some modules, including optimization, Monte Carlo analysis, and portions of the structural and CFD systems, also rely on customized analytical models and validated engineering equations. These too required significant debugging and refinement during the integration process.

To the best of my knowledge, every feature currently included in K2 has been researched extensively and validated multiple times, both from an architectural and a physics perspective. That said, no software is perfect, especially one of this scale developed by a single student. There may still be bugs, inconsistencies, or unexpected edge cases that I have not encountered.

If you come across anything suspicious, incorrect, or broken, please report it. Every report helps improve K2.

You may also notice that certain parts of the software are slower than expected. This is intentional in some places. A number of modules currently execute sequentially because they depend heavily on the outputs of previous stages, and maintaining a stable, divergence-free simulation pipeline has taken priority over raw performance. Expanding parallel execution and improving overall speed is one of the highest priorities for future releases.

K2 is open source for exactly this reason. I don't expect it to be perfect, and I believe collaborative development is the best way to make it better over time.

One final note: K2 AeroSim is not affiliated with any company or organization. There is no deeper meaning behind the name — I simply thought "K2" sounded cool and decided to stick with it.

Features Currently Under Development

  • AI-Powered Optimization Engine — automatic optimization of geometry, propulsion, stability, and performance parameters.
  • Enhanced Parallel Computing — progressive parallelization of additional modules through dependency-aware architectural refactoring for improved performance.

Updates

v0.2.0 latest

  • The Sim Flies the Rocket You Drew — every fin set, nose diameter, shoulder, boat-tail and transition in the Design tab now reaches the flight model. Values you set are never overwritten by estimates, and a finless design flies finless. The Design tab's stability margin and the simulation's now come from the same CP model.
  • Correct Component Masses — components are weighed by their own material. Some named materials (e.g. "Aluminum 6061-T6") were previously weighed as cardboard.
  • One Flutter Answer — Dynamics, the Structures fin tab and the modal panel now report one flutter speed and judge it on one SAFE / CAUTION / UNSAFE scale, against the rocket's real maximum speed, for every fin set.
  • Structures Fixes — the CalculiX static model carries the real flight loads with inertia relief; without CalculiX the fallback analyses your actual fins against their own material, and never shows a stale result from a previous run.
  • Batch & Optimizer Consistency — Monte Carlo and optimization runs match the single-flight engine (fin drag was missing), and the optimizer results panel shows the whole design.
  • Smoother UI — hidden 3D viewports no longer render in the background, docked panels stay open on tab switch, and a crash on quit from the Design tab is fixed.

v0.1.10

  • Leaner AI Assistant — the assistant now ships with two backends instead of three: Gemini (free tier, gemini-3.6-flash) as the default cloud model, and Ollama (qwen3:1.7b) for fully offline local use. The Anthropic backend was removed to keep setup simple and the download smaller.
  • Installer Fix — updates no longer stack on top of the previous release. The installer now clears the old program files before writing the new ones, so upgrading in place gives you a clean install instead of a mix of two versions.

v0.1.9

  • In-App AI Design Assistant — a dockable assistant that can read your live rocket model, explain results, and change the design through a tool bridge: edit components, run simulations and fast-forward flights on request. Pluggable LLM backends, so you can run it in the cloud or entirely on your own machine.
  • 6DOF Aero Frame Unified — the simulation engine and the batch (Monte Carlo / optimization) runner used to carry two hand-copied copies of the flight derivatives. They now share one frame, so a single-run flight and the same case inside a sweep produce identical physics.
  • Dynamics Corrections — fixed the divergence eigenvalue and rebuilt the base-excitation FRF (it was reporting receptance, not transmissibility), so vibration and aeroelastic numbers are trustworthy.
  • Structures Corrections — free-free modal analysis, rail-exit detection, and stress scaling in the viewer all fixed.
  • Physics Corrections — nozzle area ratio, fin interference K-table range, tube radii, and wind turbulence models corrected.
  • Supersonic CFD Benchmark — a Barrowman-vs-SU2 comparison added to the validation suite, plus a configurable VTK dump rate so long solves don't fill the disk.

Earlier releases

  • Liquid Propellant Simulation — liquid and hybrid rocket propulsion now supported, with a dedicated engine designer covering bi-propellant and monopropellant systems, injector and feed-system modeling, and full integration into both single-stage and multi-stage builds.
  • Multi-Stage Rocket Simulation — full multistage flight with stage separation, ignition sequencing, and stage-wise trajectory analysis through the staging manager.
  • Flight Sanity Barrier — a built-in safety monitor that watches every simulation in real time and aborts physically impossible flights instead of reporting garbage results. It catches diverged trajectories, tumbling and unstable rockets, and bad configurations, then reports a plain-language diagnosis of why the flight failed (e.g. insufficient stability margin, runaway gust torque, under-powered motor) so the problem can be fixed at the source.
  • Improved Solid Motor Customization — the solid motor builder now exposes many more parameters and a more accurate internal model, giving finer control over grain geometry and thrust behavior and producing thrust curves and performance numbers that track real motor data more closely.
